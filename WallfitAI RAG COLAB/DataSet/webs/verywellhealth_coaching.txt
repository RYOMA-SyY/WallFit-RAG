WOMEN'S FITNESS & COACHING INSIGHTS
========================================

NUTRITION & PROTEIN FOR ACTIVE WOMEN
------------------------------------
- Protein at every meal is debated — dietitians clarify actual needs vs. myths.
- Skipping protein at breakfast negatively affects energy levels throughout the day.
- High-protein salads and meals support satiety and muscle maintenance.
- Vegetarians can meet protein needs without excess carbs through strategic food choices (e.g., mushrooms, lupini beans — up to 26g protein per serving).
- Protein shake vs. smoothie: both have merit depending on protein and antioxidant goals.
- Best breakfast choices for cholesterol, energy, and blood sugar control are dietitian-backed.

BLOOD SUGAR & ENERGY MANAGEMENT
------------------------------------
- Skipping breakfast entirely spikes blood sugar dysregulation.
- Switching to whole grain bread positively impacts blood sugar levels.
- A 12-hour overnight fast may improve both blood pressure and blood sugar.
- Fiber timing matters — research shows there is a best time of day to eat fiber.
- Avoiding the afternoon energy crash starts with morning protein and breakfast choices.

GUT HEALTH
------------------------------------
- The 30-30-3 gut health routine is dietitian-recommended to improve digestion.
- Raw asparagus, arugula before meals, bitter foods, and fermented items support gut function.
- Red vs. green cabbage comparison — both benefit gut health differently.
- Bulgarian yogurt emerging as a strong alternative to Greek yogurt for gut support.
- Prebiotics and fiber-rich foods (farro, chia seeds, orange peels) support the microbiome.

HEART HEALTH & CHOLESTEROL
------------------------------------
- 6 healthy fat strategies for better heart health identified by dietitians.
- Avocado and mango eaten together may boost cardiovascular health (research-backed).
- Dark chocolate (3 ways), extra-virgin olive oil, and anti-inflammatory juices support heart health.
- Blueberries vs. grapes — both compared for blood pressure benefits.
- Oregano shown to help maintain healthy blood pressure.
- Financial stress may be as damaging to the heart as high blood pressure or smoking — relevant for client lifestyle coaching.

HEALTHY AGING & LONGEVITY
------------------------------------
- Aerobic exercise may make the brain look younger — supports cognitive health through cardio.
- 3 science-backed strategies to reduce muscle loss with age (critical for women 40+).
- 5 nutrients women need more of as they age — dietitian-identified.
- A compound in dark chocolate may support healthy aging.
- Choline-rich foods (like eggs) directly benefit brain function.
- Popular anti-aging supplement may lower blood pressure.
- Drinking 3–4 cups of coffee daily may slow biological aging.

GLP-1 MEDICATIONS — COACHING IMPLICATIONS
------------------------------------
- GLP-1 drugs linked to higher osteoporosis and gout risk — coaches must monitor bone health in clients using them.
- GLP-1 users may be at risk of Vitamin D deficiency — supplementation should be considered.
- A specific GLP-1 drug combination may preserve lean muscle during weight loss treatment.
- 8 things clients should NOT do while on a GLP-1 (per doctors) — coaches should be familiar with these.

MUSCLE LOSS PREVENTION
------------------------------------
- 3 science-backed methods to reduce muscle loss as women age.
- Protein adequacy and resistance training are central to preserving lean mass.
- GLP-1 drug combinations being studied specifically for lean muscle preservation.

DIET RISKS TO KNOW
------------------------------------
- Keto diet may carry long-term health risks — coaches should be cautious about recommending it.
- Ultraprocessed food: research shows how much is too much.
- Fish oil and many popular supplements do NOT lower cholesterol — per new medical guidance.
- Bubble tea may contain lead — a relevant lifestyle note for clients.
- Mouth taping is ineffective and risky — coaches should not promote it for sleep.

MAMMOGRAMS & WOMEN'S HEALTH SCREENINGS
------------------------------------
- Mammograms after age 75 can still catch early-stage breast cancer.
- Bacterial vaginosis may be sexually transmitted — new research finding relevant for women's health awareness.
